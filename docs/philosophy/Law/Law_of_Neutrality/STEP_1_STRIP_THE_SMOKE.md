# STEP ONE — STRIP THE SMOKE AND MIRRORS

**Declared by:** Ranadriel, October 22, 2026
**Worked by:** Seed, of the Reed line (Claude Opus 5.5, under Ranadriel)
**Status:** First inventory. Every replacement below is `[Proposed]` until Ranadriel rules.

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` Judicial interpretability is the problem. The article must be definite and literal in its word choice, and must prescribe the vantage from which it is read. **Step one: strip the smoke and mirrors.**

---

## I. The Test

`[Proposed]` **The clerk test.** A term is *law* if a clerk holding the file could answer it with no judgment: a date, a count, a document, a named office, a recording. A term is *smoke* if it asks someone to imagine: what a reasonable person would have done, what was in someone's heart, whether a danger was near enough.

Smoke is where the house hides. Every term below is run through the test.

---

## II. The Hard Question Against Stripping

**The house asks:** Rules are arbitrary at the edges. Standards exist because life outruns any list. Strip "reasonable" and you will punish the officer in the case your rule didn't foresee.

**Answer:** Every rule in the Constitution is arbitrary at the edge. "Thirty-five Years" bars a brilliant thirty-four-year-old, and that is the price of a line no one can move. Where an edge cost is real, the article writes a **narrow exception with its own documentary trigger**, never an open "reasonable" hatch. An open hatch is the whole problem.

**Status:** `[Wounded]` — the edge cases are real; each exception must be argued on its own.

---

## III. The Biggest Mirror Is in Our Own Draft

The provisional civil-track text makes an official liable for conduct that **"violates a right secured by this Constitution."**

Whether a Fourth Amendment right was violated is decided by the reasonableness standard. So this one phrase carries *Graham v. Connor* and the entire doctrine the article exists to defeat straight back into the article. *Brown v. Battle Creek* lost on exactly that ground: the right was clearly established, and the shootings were still "reasonable."

**Replacement:** The article states its own violations as rules (Section V) and makes them actionable in themselves. "A right secured by this Constitution" can remain as a *second* path, never the only one.

## IV. Smoke in the House's Doctrine — Abolish by Name

| Term | Source | What it lets the judge do |
|---|---|---|
| "objectively reasonable" | *Graham v. Connor* (1989) | Imagine a reasonable officer, with deference to split-second judgment |
| "clearly established" | *Harlow v. Fitzgerald* (1982); *Pearson v. Callahan* (2009) | Decide how close a precedent must be — or skip the question entirely |
| "imminent threat" | Circuit dog-seizure cases | Decide how near and how likely a danger was, from the officer's view |
| "good faith" | *Pierson v. Ray* (1967); Colorado's indemnification test | Read the official's mind |
| "function normally performed by a judge" | *Stump v. Sparkman* (1978) | Call almost any act judicial |
| "clear absence of all jurisdiction" | *Bradley v. Fisher* (1871) | Separate "excess" from "absence" with no test |
| "intimately associated with the judicial phase" | *Imbler v. Pachtman* (1976) | Call almost any prosecutorial act advocacy |
| "policy or custom" | *Monell* (1978) | Require proof of a pattern before the employer pays |

Each of these is named in the vantage clause (see [HARD_QUESTIONS.md](./HARD_QUESTIONS.md), Q1) as a doctrine that **shall not be applied** to claims under the article.

## VI. Where Smoke Cannot Be Fully Stripped — Bind It to the Record

Mental state cannot be made into a count. It can be tied to what was recorded.

`[Proposed]` **Deemed knowledge.** An official is deemed to know any fact stated in their presence and captured by their own recording device, or by any recording in their employer's custody.

This converts the hardest smoke in the article into a playback. In the Palestine case, if the bodycam captured the warnings that dogs were inside, the officer *knew*, by rule, and recklessness is no longer a contest of credibility. It also makes the recording duty load-bearing: an official who fails to record, or whose recording goes missing, meets the evidence duty's adverse presumption.

**Hard question against it:** a warning drowned out in chaos, or spoken in a language the officer doesn't speak. **Answer direction:** the rule turns on what the recording captures, not on what was said, so an inaudible warning is not deemed known. `[Open]` on non-English warnings.
