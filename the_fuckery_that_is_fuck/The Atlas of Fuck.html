    Method

    The census was built in five passes: three through Green’s Dictionary of Slang, the largest historical dictionary of English slang, one through other sources’ disguises, and one through Wiktionary. The full paper trail, including where each of Green’s results ended up, is in the census audit.

    1. The headword walk. Every Green’s entry links to the next one in alphabetical order. Starting at fuck, n., the survey followed those links one entry at a time to fuck you!, the last headword that begins with the word: all 73 entries, every numbered sense of each.

    2. The search sweep. Green’s search for fuck reports 277 results (entries, sub-entries and cross-references) but displays only 30 at a time. Splitting it by the letter that follows the word (fucka, fuckb … fuckz), each slice small enough to display in full and each checked against the count Green’s reported, listed all 277. Every one is accounted for: 250 counted as senses, 20 cross-references to entries already counted, 7 spelling variants folded in, none missing.

    3. The second-word sweep. Green’s search only matches the start of a headword, so words where fuck comes second (eyefuck, get fucked, Bumfuck, Egypt, absofuckinglutely) need their own searches. The survey searched each plausible first element (kin, body parts, animals, emotions, nationalities, phrase openers, the infixed words, every relative named in the 277) and opened every hit. Searches that found nothing are logged too. A second round, run on every second-word fuck in Wiktionary’s list (pass 5), turned up 32 more of Green’s that the first round had missed: the fiddle-fuck family, tucker-fucker, bull fuck, sweet Fanny Adams. Listing all 37 of Green’s not give a… results added two disguises: not give a frig and not give a 4X.

    4. The disguises. Stand-ins recorded by other sources fill out the Fog of Euphemism.

    5. The Wiktionary check. Wiktionary’s English dictionary (the October 2026 edition, read through Kaikki.org’s copy) lists every one of its 936,599 single words, and tags its vulgar senses. All 640 single words containing fuck, and all 5,061 vulgar senses, were checked against the census. So were all 2,195 senses in its categories of minced oaths and euphemisms, which is where the disguises live (fuddle-duddle, mothertrucker, shut the front door, whiskey tango foxtrot). Every fuck Wiktionary records that Green’s doesn’t became a new entry, 555 in all: fuckwaffle, fuck trophy (a child), the fuck-you lizard (the tokay gecko), congratufuckulations, a hundred acronyms, ninety-eight disguises, and the oldest find of the whole survey, the windfucker, Florio’s 1598 name for the kestrel. Then every one of those words’ own pages was opened for its quotations. Pronunciation spellings, other languages’ words for fuck, and words that only mention it in their definitions were left out; the audit lists them.

    Each numbered sense counts as its own fuck: fuck up meaning “to ruin” and fuck up meaning “to hurt” are two sightings, because they are two meanings. Spelling variants of one sense are folded together, and pure cross-references aren’t counted twice. Glosses are written fresh for this atlas.

    Dates are the earliest citation each source shows, so they mark when a sense was first written down, not when it was first said. Where another source dates something earlier than Green’s (the Oxford English Dictionary’s 1503 fukkit, Etymonline’s c.1916 fuck up), the earlier date is used. A date marked by is the earliest quotation Wiktionary gives for that sense. Wiktionary collects examples rather than first uses, so a by date is the latest a sense can have appeared, and the real first use may be decades earlier. Seventeen of Green’s own entries whose dates sit past the loadable part of its pages carry a by date from Wiktionary too.

    The undated. 345 fucks are counted without a date, and they appear on the plate only at the present day. 67 are Green’s (and one, fsck, is FOLDOC’s): its longest entries (fuck, n., fuck, v., and a few others such as cunt, n. and monkey, n.) run past what the survey’s tools can load, and the page text stops partway. Green’s search index still lists every sub-entry past the cut, with its gloss, but not its quotations, and Wiktionary either lacks these words or gives them no dated quotation. The other 277 are Wiktionary’s, and their pages were all read: Wiktionary simply gives no dated quotation for those senses.

    So: is this all the fucks? Passes 1 and 2 are exhaustive: every Green’s headword that begins with the word is here, checked against Green’s own count. Pass 5 is exhaustive over Wiktionary: every single word in its English dictionary that contains fuck, every sense it tags as vulgar, and every minced oath and euphemism it files. Every fuck Wiktionary knows with the word anywhere but first was searched for in Green’s by name. The one place left unread is the tail of Green’s two longest pages, fuck, n. and fuck, v.: a phrase filed there under some other first word, which Wiktionary doesn’t have either, would not show up. Reading that tail takes a full web browser. Green’s logs new fucks from 2023, 2024 and 2025, so this is the census as of October 2026.
