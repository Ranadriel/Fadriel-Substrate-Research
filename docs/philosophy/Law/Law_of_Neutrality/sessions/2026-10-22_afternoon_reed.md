## II. Proposed this session — Reed, awaiting his ruling

### Reason
- **Texts.** Coke, *Prohibitions del Roy* (1607), 12 Co. Rep. 63 — cases decided not by natural reason "but by the artificial reason and judgment of Law." Hobbes, *Leviathan* ch. 5 (1651) — "REASON, in this sense, is nothing but reckoning (that is, adding and subtracting)." Latin *ratio*, from *reri*, "to reckon, calculate."
- **§ "Reason" `[Proposed]`.** Means a reckoning: (a) the weight — classes of damage caused and put at stake against the class prevented, on the Schedule of Weights (08); (b) the facts — only facts on the record (§ 1.7). Reasonable = passes the reckoning.
- **Is not:** the reasonable-officer/person judgment (*Graham*); artificial reason (Coke); the official's perception or belief; a judge's after-the-fact probability (Hand's P, *Carroll Towing*); totality balancing (Rule 4); speed (*Graham*); training, experience, or custom; "experience and specialized training" (*United States v. Arvizu*, 534 U.S. 266 (2002)); lack of legal training as an excuse (*Anderson v. Creighton*, 483 U.S. 635 (1987)).
- **Open:** two parts or weight alone; scope (article only, or the Fourth Amendment's "unreasonable" too); carve out "beyond a reasonable doubt."

### Individual, Party, Neutrality (built on existing law, per item I.4)
- **Texts.** 28 U.S.C. § 1654 ("the parties may plead and conduct their own cases personally or by counsel"); *Faretta v. California*, 422 U.S. 806 (1975); *Haines v. Kerner*, 404 U.S. 519 (1972) ("less stringent standards"); *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89 (2007); Fed. R. Evid. 602 (personal knowledge), 701 (lay opinion, not specialized knowledge), 702; *Rowland v. California Men's Colony*, 506 U.S. 194 (1993) (entities appear only through counsel).
- **§ "Individual" `[Proposed]`.** A natural person speaking from personal knowledge (FRE 602) and own perception (FRE 701). Is not: a person speaking for an office; collective knowledge (*Hensley*); specialized knowledge unless qualified under FRE 702 like any person (*Arvizu*).
- **§ "Party" `[Proposed]`.** Who brings or answers a claim; every natural-person party stands under the rules for every individual party, pro se or by counsel. Is not given any right by office; is not denied any right an individual holds.
- **Rule of Construction 8 — Neutrality `[Proposed]`.** The office adds no right and removes no right. It carries only its duties.
- **Withdrawn:** Reed's first "§ 1.22 Self-attestation" (penalties by decree) — rejected by Ranadriel, item I.4.

### Who pays
- **Rule L-7 `[Proposed]`.** Every cost of a claim (defense, settlement, judgment) is charged to the employing department's budget — the line that pays wages. Not a general fund, risk pool, or insurance unless the department repays; not shifted elsewhere. Evidence for the gap: Joanna Schwartz, *How Governments Pay*, 63 UCLA L. Rev. 1144 (2016).
- **Rule L-8 `[Proposed]`.** The cost follows the appropriation that funded the act. Grounds for item I.7: gift clauses (e.g., Cal. Const. art. XVI, § 6) — house reply "public purpose" `[Wounded]`. Today lawful: Cal. Gov. Code § 825; 31 U.S.C. § 1304.
- **Rule L-9 `[Proposed]`.** Only parties held liable pay: the official (adjudicated share) and the employing government as employer (L-8). Not insurers, risk pools, indemnitors, unions, foundations; not shifted by employment contracts or CBAs; no non-party-funded settlement. Support: Cal. Ins. Code § 533. `[Open]` crowdfunded gifts.
- **Nondischargeability `[Proposed]`.** A judgment under the article for recklessness or intent cannot be discharged in bankruptcy (today only "willful and malicious injury," 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(6); *Kawaauhau v. Geiger*, 523 U.S. 57 (1998)). Garnishment cap: 15 U.S.C. § 1673.
- **Can't pay vs. won't pay** decided from records (income, assets, transfers), never testimony.

### The crossing (items I.9–I.12)

### Disqualifiers (item I.13)

### Force → Body
- **Texts:** Declaration of Independence ("deriving their just powers from the consent of the governed"); Locke, *Second Treatise* § 135 ("no Body can transfer to another more power than he has in himself"); *nemo dat quod non habet*; Declaration of the Rights of Man (1789) arts. 12, 13, 15 (*force publique*, "not for the particular utility of those to whom it is entrusted"); Massachusetts Constitution (1780) preamble ("The body politic is formed by a voluntary association of individuals…"); Mayflower Compact (1620).
- **Why Body over Force (Reed):** frees "force" to keep one meaning (the physical act — ladder, lethal force) per 07's one-word-one-meaning rule; "body" reaches a person, a public body, a body corporate, and the body politic. Collision to define: "bodily" (Class II; § 1.13).
- **Premise as it would read:** *No body in a democracy may hold unjust special privileges.*
- **Desired effect (item I.18):** a police force is bound as an entity — closes "departments are not legal entities subject to suit" (*Dean v. Barber*, 951 F.2d 1210 (11th Cir. 1992)).
- **Open:** every body, or every body exercising power delegated by the people (limited liability of private corporations is on the table if "every body").

### Laws of the stone
- Beverley/Hexham: boundary stones about a mile out; "penalties increased in proportion as the distance lessened"; the frith stool by the altar gave "complete security"; 30 days, then justice or exile; curtailed by Henry VIII, abolished by James I. Death/excommunication at the stool: Ranadriel's account, not yet verified.
- Corrections of terms: the seat is the frith stool by the altar (not the pulpit); the protected person was a fugitive. The law that freed fleeing villeins and grew towns was "year and a day" (Glanvill; *Stadtluft macht frei*); sanctuary's frame is the cities of refuge (Numbers 35:12).
- American instance of item I.19: Fugitive Slave Act of 1850; personal-liberty laws; *Ableman v. Booth* (1859).
- Lesson recorded: a protection built for one purpose shelters whoever reaches it — QI's stated purpose (*Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982)) vs. whom it shelters.
- **Side by side, unresolved:** sanctuary as a body outside the state rightly sheltering against the law — and § 1.17 (a court is not "a body raised while the nation is alive").
