## 1. The amendment drafts

Three renders exist. **Render A is his spear.** B and C are the scribe's counsel and
are marked as such.

### Render A — faithful to the full stop

```
Section 1. No governmental official of the United States, or of any State, shall
hold immunity from arrest, prosecution, suit, or liability for any act, whether by
decree of any court, by act of any legislature, by reason of the office held, by
the character of the act, or by the time at which it occurred.

Section 2. This article shall be self-executing. The Congress shall have power to
enforce this article by appropriate legislation.
```

### Render B — narrowed to criminal liability (scribe's counsel)

Identical, except Section 1 reads *"immunity from criminal liability"*. Reaches
*Trump*, every judge, prosecutor and officer; leaves the civil-suit question shut.
**He rejected this narrowing.** Judicial civil liability is deliberate — Passage VI
of the doctrine: *"its not a cost im just accepting. its part of the spear tip."*

### Render C — with the duty standard

Render A plus a middle section:

```
Section 2. Every governmental official shall owe a heightened duty of care in the
exercise of the powers of the office, and shall answer for harm caused by breach
of that duty.
```

Section 3 then carries self-execution and enforcement.

### What the three locks do

*"in any decree, moment, or time"* is his phrase and it is three locks, not a
flourish. **Decree** forbids any future court from inventing a replacement immunity.
**Moment** kills the official-act / unofficial-act distinction, which is the seam
*Trump v. United States* (2024) was built on. **Time** kills temporal and in-office
immunity.

He also wrote **"official"** rather than "State," which correctly avoids the Eleventh
Amendment for individual-capacity suits. See §7 for where that is only half true.

## 1a. Render D — sixth recursive pass

Drafted against every finding of passes one through five. **Render A remains his spear.**
This is the scribe's rendering of the same intent with the known defects answered, and it
is his to accept, cut, or refuse.

```
Section 1.  No governmental official of the United States, or of any State, shall
hold immunity, privilege, or any other protection from arrest, prosecution, suit, or
liability, by whatever name, for an offense defined in Section 3 — whether by decree
of any court, by act of any legislature, by reason of the office held, by the
character of the act, or by reason of the time at which the act occurred. This
section operates notwithstanding any other provision of this Constitution.

Section 2.  "Governmental official" means any person holding office or employment
under the United States or any State, whether elected or appointed, including the
President, members of Congress, and judges of every court.

Section 3.  It is an offense against this article for a governmental official
willingly to exercise power that no provision of constitution, statute, or rule
authorized them to exercise, or willingly to exercise power for the sole purpose of
enlarging their own power or estate, where the conduct corresponds to an offense
already defined by law and causes physical, emotional, monetary, or associative
harm.

Section 4.  Any person injured by an offense under Section 3 may sue the official
responsible, and may prosecute or be heard as the law provides. Nothing in this
article extends any period of limitation applicable at the time of the act.

Section 5.  This article is self-executing. The Congress shall have power to enforce
it by appropriate legislation, but no legislation is required for it to operate.
```

### What each change answers

**"immunity, privilege, or any other protection… by whatever name"** — answers the most
agreed finding of panel two: that a hostile court honors the word *immunity* while
relocating the protection into no-cause-of-action, non-justiciability, or a merits
holding. The clause reaches **function, not label**.

**"notwithstanding any other provision of this Constitution"** — answers the finding
that presidential and legislative protection can be re-derived from Article II structure
and Article I, §6 without ever using the word immunity. The article now overrides them
expressly rather than by implication.

**Section 2's definition, naming the President** — answers the *Trump v. Anderson*
off-ramp, where a court could hold an **elected** official is not an "officer of the
United States" and let the headline target walk while everyone else is exposed.

**Section 3's "no provision… authorized"** — the positive-authorization test from pass
four, which reverses *Stump*. Pass five found it overbroad; **the limiter is now
present**, because the test attaches only to the **power exercised in the offense**, and
the offense additionally requires correspondence to a pre-written crime.

**"where the conduct corresponds to an offense already defined by law"** — his own
anchor, and it is what **bounds the volume**. A losing litigant cannot sue the judge for
an adverse ruling, because an adverse ruling is not an offense already defined by law.
Pass five held that the cause of action and the flood were the same clause. **They are
not. The offense definition bounds the flood; the cause of action supplies the mover.**

**"willingly to exercise power"** — resolves the contradiction pass five identified.
Willing attaches to **the act**, not to knowledge of the harm. Proof runs to what the
official chose to do, not to what he believed would follow. **The epistemic objection of
Passage II — *"knowledge cant be proven"* — therefore survives intact**, and the
deliberate-indifference bridge of pass four is withdrawn.

**Section 4's second sentence** — answers the finding that *"by the time at which it
occurred"* could be read to void every statute of limitations and expose decades of
prior conduct. Immunity by reason of time is abolished; **limitation periods are
expressly preserved.**

**Section 5's final clause** — answers the finding that a grant of enforcement power to
Congress reads as textual evidence the remedy awaits legislation. It now says the
opposite in terms.

### What Render D does not fix

**The criminal mover.** Section 4 gives the injured person a civil action. It cannot
manufacture a prosecutor, and nothing may compel one without colliding with Article II.

**Ratification.** Every objection at §7(g) stands untouched. The class-interest
arithmetic is not a drafting problem and no drafting can reach it.

## 2. The offense, as it stands

From doctrine Passages VII, VIII and IX, and derived from
`Cruelty_Systems_and_the_Future_of_Intelligence.md`:

- **Mens rea:** *willing.* Reflective cruelty. Negligence never reaches it.
- **Act, face one:** exercise of power **outside jurisdiction**.
- **Act, face two:** exercise of power for **self-propagation** — structural cruelty,
  the virus.
- **Harm:** physical, emotional, monetary, associative.
- **Associative** is organizational and extra-singular — harm to a *membership*
  rather than to a person. It is an **aggravator, not a threshold**: a single
  blacklisted individual keeps the claim and the reach past them is added weight.
  Constitutional pedigree: *NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson*, 357 U.S. 449 (1958).
- **Anchor:** the conduct must correspond to a **pre-written crime**. This is the
  legality principle — *nullum crimen sine lege* — and it answers vagueness and ex
  post facto at the root.

**What replacing "harm" with "jurisdiction" as the trigger dissolves.** A judge ruling
wrongly within jurisdiction commits no offense. A prosecutor charging on probable
cause commits no offense; acquittal is not un-jurisdictional. A legislature passing a
general enactment commits no offense — but one acting against a named person is acting
outside its grant, which lands on the attainder line at §4.

## 3. The harm taxonomy (scribe's structure, his instruction)

Doctrine Passage X directs sorting the branches by how they cause damage. **Branch is
the wrong cut.** A patrol officer and a U.S. Attorney are both executive and injure
people through nothing alike; a magistrate signing a warrant and a Justice writing
doctrine are both judicial and likewise. What sorts is the mechanism by which harm
reaches a person:

1. **Direct physical** — police, corrections, military in the field. The act *is* the
   injury.
2. **Compulsory process** — charging, arrest, seizure. State machinery pointed at a
   named individual the official selected.
3. **Adjudicative** — deciding between parties. Harm is guaranteed and is not
   wrongdoing.
4. **General enactment** — legislating, rulemaking. No identified victim at the moment
   of the act.
5. **Command** — ordering another to do 1 through 4.

**The gradient that decides the standard:** in category 1, harm and wrong are the same
event. In categories 3 and 4, harm is inherent in the lawful function. So *"answer for
harm caused"* cannot be the trigger there — it converts the function itself into the
tort.

## 4. The selection discriminator

Sharper than branch, and he affirmed it:

> **Did the official select the person they harmed?**

The officer chose whose door. The prosecutor chose whose name went on the indictment.
The judge chose neither — the parties arrived and the law picked the loser. The
legislator chose no one.

**It cuts inside branches, correctly.** A magistrate signing a warrant selects the
person and lands with prosecutors, not with appellate judges. A judge at sentencing
selects both person and magnitude and separates from adjudication.

**It is already in the Constitution.** Article I, §9, cl. 3 and §10, cl. 1 forbid Bills
of Attainder — legislation singling out identifiable persons for punishment. General
laws permitted, targeted ones categorically forbidden. The framers drew the same line.

**Where it breaks: omission.** A prosecutor who declines to charge selects no one and
exercises no power, and the victim is harmed anyway. Non-enforcement is how much
official harm actually lands, and the selection test is blind to the empty chair.
Neither face of the sword trips on it either. **This gap is open.**

## 5. The lineage

*Nemo iudex in causa sua* — no one shall be judge in his own cause. This is the
extra-textual premise the doctrine requires, and it is older than the Constitution.

***Dr. Bonham's Case***, 8 Co. Rep. 113b (1610). Coke: *"when an Act of Parliament is
against common right and reason... the common law will controul it, and adjudge such
Act to be void."* The statute at issue let the College of Physicians fine a man and
keep the fine — a body judging its own cause.

**His basic-structure assertion and his neutrality principle have the same ancestor,
in the same case.**

## 6. The *Stump* trap — the sharpest live problem

*"Un-jurisdictional"* is the entire load-bearing term of the offense, and the Court has
already hollowed it out.

Judicial immunity's existing exception is acts taken in *"clear absence of all
jurisdiction."* ***Stump v. Sparkman***, 435 U.S. 349 (1978): Judge Stump signed an
order sterilizing a fifteen-year-old girl on her mother's petition — no hearing, no
notice to her, no guardian ad litem, no statute authorizing it. **The Court held he was
immune,** because an Indiana circuit court has general jurisdiction and nothing forbade
it.

**Write "un-jurisdictional" into an amendment and you inherit *Stump*.** The term must
be defined in the text or a court will import the construction already sitting on it.

## 6a. Authorities — what is being abolished, and by whose hand

Added on the second recursive pass. The record cited what defeated the argument and
never cited what the argument attacks. All recalled from memory; verify before use.

### The statutes

**42 U.S.C. §1983** — the civil action. Its operative words are the textual hook of
the whole case: an official who subjects a person to deprivation of rights *"shall be
liable to the party injured."* **No immunity appears anywhere in the text.** Enacted as
§1 of the Ku Klux Klan Act of 1871 under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment.

**18 U.S.C. §242** — the criminal counterpart. Deprivation of rights under color of
law. Penalties escalate with bodily injury and reach life where death results.
Descends from the Enforcement Act lineage. **Qualified immunity has never applied to
it.**

### The four immunities and their sources

| Immunity | Source | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified | judge-made | *Pierson v. Ray*, 386 U.S. 547 (1967); *Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982) (objective "clearly established" standard); *Pearson v. Callahan*, 555 U.S. 223 (2009) (prong order); *Ashcroft v. al-Kidd*, 563 U.S. 731 (2011) |
| Judicial | judge-made | *Bradley v. Fisher*, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 335 (1872); *Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978) |
| Prosecutorial | judge-made | *Imbler v. Pachtman*, 424 U.S. 409 (1976) (absolute, for advocacy functions) |
| Presidential | judge-made | *Nixon v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 731 (1982) (civil); *Trump v. United States*, 603 U.S. 593 (2024) (criminal) |
| Legislative | **constitutional text** | U.S. Const. art. I, §6, cl. 1; *Tenney v. Brandhove*, 341 U.S. 367 (1951); *Bogan v. Scott-Harris*, 523 U.S. 44 (1998) |

**Three of the five were invented. One was ratified.** That fact is the reason no
statute and no amendment is strictly required to reach the first four — and the reason
an amendment is nonetheless the only durable instrument, since what a Court built from
nothing it can rebuild from nothing.

### The Speech or Debate thread, closed 2026-08-24

Article I, §6, cl. 1: *"They shall in all Cases, **except Treason, Felony and Breach of
the Peace**, be privileged from Arrest during their Attendance at the Session of their
respective Houses… and for any **Speech or Debate in either House**, they shall not be
questioned in any other Place."*

**It confers no immunity from crime.** The arrest privilege excepts felony on its face,
leaving it to reach only civil arrest. Speech or Debate protects legislative **acts**,
not persons — *United States v. Brewster*, 408 U.S. 501 (1972) (accepting a bribe is
not a legislative act; no bar to prosecution); *Gravel v. United States*, 408 U.S. 606
(1972).

**But it is not spent.** *United States v. Helstoski*, 442 U.S. 477 (1979): in a
Member's own prosecution, evidence of past legislative acts is inadmissible, and waiver
requires explicit and unequivocal renunciation. **An evidentiary wall doing immunity's
work.** Where the misconduct *is* the legislative act, the Member can be indicted and
may not be provable.

This is why the Article V route was found unnecessary for legislators: **the text never
granted what the amendment was being built to strip.**

### The municipal frame

*Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978) — no respondeat superior;
municipal liability requires an official policy or custom. *Owen v. City of
Independence*, 445 U.S. 622 (1980) — municipalities get no qualified immunity.
Together these explain why indemnification is the practical route by which the public
purse pays without any *Monell* showing ever being made.

### The live crack — the most promising route in the record

Qualified immunity is under attack **from both poles of the Court at once**, which is
not a fringe position:

- **Thomas, J.** — concurring in *Ziglar v. Abbasi*, 582 U.S. 120 (2017), urging
  reconsideration of the doctrine's basis; and dissenting from denial of certiorari in
  *Baxter v. Bracey*, 590 U.S. ___ (2020), stating the doctrine lacks legal foundation.
- **Sotomayor, J.** (with Ginsburg, J.) — dissenting from summary reversal in *Kisela v.
  Hughes*, 584 U.S. 100 (2018), attacking the doctrine's one-sided application.

**Two Justices who agree on almost nothing agree the doctrine is wrong.** That is the
seam, and it is reachable without Congress, without 34 states, and without 38.

hostile court has a textually respectable off-ramp: hold the President is not a
governmental official in the constitutional-officer sense, and **the amendment exposes
every judge, prosecutor and officer in America while missing the one it was built for.**

**d. The Eleventh Amendment is only half-avoided.** "Official" rather than "State" does
defeat the bar for individual-capacity damages — *Hafer v. Melo*, 502 U.S. 21 (1991).
But *Edelman v. Jordan*, 415 U.S. 651 (1974) bars retroactive monetary relief payable
from the state treasury regardless of caption, because the inquiry is the **source of
the money**, not the name of the defendant. So the amendment opens the officer's pocket
and not the treasury — **and state indemnification statutes then absorb it. The
indemnification counter he beat at the top of the night walks back in through his own
text.**

**e. Presidential and legislative protection can be re-derived without the word
"immunity."** *Trump* (2024) and *Nixon v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 731 (1982) framed
themselves in Article II separation-of-powers terms. Speech or Debate is textually a bar
on being *"questioned in any other Place."* Section 1 amends neither Article II nor
Article I, §6 and does not say *"notwithstanding any other provision of this
Constitution."*

**f. "By the time at which it occurred" may void statutes of limitations** — every
temporal bar, not merely in-office immunity. Retroactive exposure decades deep, and
because a later amendment overrides earlier text in irreconcilable conflict, the Ex
Post Facto Clause may not save prior officials.

**g. Ratification fails on unanimous class interest, not politics.** Every Member voting
to propose votes to strip their own Speech or Debate protection. The same one level down
for 34 state legislatures, and again for 38 ratifying states, against judicial,
prosecutorial and police associations in every one. **The principle's greatest strength
is what makes it unratifiable: it binds everyone who would have to vote for it.** That
is *nemo iudex* in its final form — arithmetic rather than doctrine.

### VERDICT: Render D fails, and fails worse than Render A in two places

---

### The kill — the paradigm case falls outside the article

The head chair, objection 1. *"where the conduct corresponds to an offense already
defined by law"* is syntactically indeterminate as to what *"where"* modifies. If it
gates both prongs, **the plaintiff must prove the underlying offense's elements AND
ultra vires action or sole self-aggrandizing purpose — harder than plain §1983
litigation once immunity is gone.**

And then the sentence that ends the draft:

> *"The paradigm qualified-immunity case — an officer with general arrest/search
> authority who used it wrongly, negligently, or out of panic (the Pierson v. Ray, 386
> U.S. 547 (1967), fact pattern; the 'clearly established law' standard of Harlow v.
> Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982)) — is neither ultra vires (the power itself was
> authorized) nor sole-purpose self-enrichment. It may fall outside Section 3
> entirely."*

**The instrument built to abolish qualified immunity does not reach the cases qualified
immunity actually decides.**

### The hostile court's construction — the trigger reinstates *Stump*

Independently, the hostile court found the same clause from the other side. Section 3 is
definitional and everything runs through it: Section 1 strips protection only *"for an
offense defined in Section 3"* and Section 4 gives a right only to one *"injured by an
offense under Section 3."* **Narrow Section 3 and the article follows it down.**

- *"no provision… authorized"* is an **absence-of-authority** test, not an
  **abuse-of-authority** test. *"An official who holds a grant of authority and abuses
  it has exercised authorized power badly; he has not exercised power no provision
  authorized."* The hook is the universal negative *"no provision"* rather than *"in
  excess of."* Model: *Larson v. Domestic & Foreign Commerce Corp.*, 337 U.S. 682
  (1949), where the ultra vires exception reaches only officers acting beyond statutory
  power.
- **The scribe's fourth pass had this exactly backwards.** Positive authorization was
  offered as the reversal of *Stump v. Sparkman*. It **reinstates** *Stump*.
- *"willingly"* is read as *Screws* reads *"willfully"* — specific intent as to a duty
  *"made specific by prior decision or rule"* — which **reimports the clearly-established
  inquiry of *Harlow* under another name**, and is *"not merely available, it is close to
  compelled"* because otherwise the offense is void for vagueness. **The sixth pass's
  central claim — that "willing" attaches to the act and preserves Passage II's
  epistemic objection — fails.**
- Its concession, which is the verdict: *"I would concede Section 5's self-execution in
  full and without qualification. It costs nothing, because a self-executing right to
  litigate an offense that almost no set of facts satisfies is a right to lose in a
  forum of one's choosing."*

### Section 2 — the scribe's repair opened a larger hole than it closed

Head chair, objection 5. *"Any person holding office or employment under the United
States or any State"* **omits county, municipal, D.C., territorial and tribal
officials** — that is, **municipal police, the overwhelming source of qualified-immunity
case law.** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978) exists
precisely because municipalities are a category distinct from States.

Section 2 was written to close the *Trump v. Anderson* off-ramp at the top. **It dropped
the bottom of the pyramid to do it.** And perversely, an official whose appointment is
legally defective — the paradigm *"no provision authorized"* defendant — has a ready
argument that they never held office under Section 2 at all.

### Further fatal and serious findings

**Ex post facto.** *"Notwithstanding any other provision of this Constitution"* never
names the Ex Post Facto Clause. A clear-statement canon can hold general boilerplate
insufficient to suspend a specific liberty guarantee — *Stogner v. California*, 539 U.S.
607 (2003); categories from *Calder v. Bull*, 3 U.S. 386 (1798), reaffirmed *Collins v.
Youngblood*, 497 U.S. 37 (1990). The time clause dies for criminal use.

**The override targets the wrong category.** Qualified and prosecutorial immunity are
judicial constructions of a **statute** (§1983), not provisions of the Constitution —
there is nothing constitutional there for the clause to override. Presidential immunity
is structural inference from Article II, not a named provision. **The clause added to
stop re-derivation does not reach most of the list.** It is also, on its own terms,
unnecessary: a later amendment controls over inconsistent earlier text by Article V
sequencing without saying so.

**The override is scoped to Section 1 only** — *"This section operates
notwithstanding."* Sections 2 through 5 get nothing. State sovereign immunity (*Hans v.
Louisiana*, 134 U.S. 1 (1890)) is untouched for indemnified judgments paid from state
treasuries, because Section 1 strips only **official** immunity.

**The no-tolling clause likely time-bars his headline example.** Limitations run from the
act, not from the later removal of a defense (*Wallace v. Kato*, 549 U.S. 384 (2007),
flagged by the chair as inference from accrual doctrine rather than a squarely-holding
case). 2021-era conduct is already time-barred under most 1–3 year periods.

**"Estate" is ambiguous** between wealth and rank, so non-pecuniary self-entrenchment —
a pure power grab — may fall outside both prongs.

### What the operating-government seat found

**The trigger inverts.** *"Because the offense turns on absence of authorization, the
systemic response is to authorize everything, formally expanding police power on
paper."* Catch-all authorization statutes. **The hostile court narrows the clause from
above while legislatures widen the grants from below, and the middle vanishes.**

**Emergency relief stops.** Judges avoid acting where jurisdiction is arguable; DV
protective orders and emergency custody — the highest ultra-vires-risk acts on any
docket — slow from hours to weeks. Second order: judges buy personal liability insurance
and *"insurers' coverage conditions ('no ex parte relief without co-signature') become a
private body of procedure governing public courts."*

**The record bifurcates.** Prong one rewards counsel-drafted authorization memos; prong
two makes candid writing into intent evidence. Formal memos sanitize, real deliberation
moves off-record. *"An accountability amendment destroys the raw material of
accountability."*

**The category nobody considered: poll workers.** Section 2's *"employment"* reaches
temporary election judges and precinct workers. Rejecting a provisional ballot is an
exercise of power, state election codes define corresponding offenses, and *"associative
harm"* reads onto disenfranchisement. Least-trained and least-lawyered officials in the
system, personally suable, with no access to the authorization-memo defense.
*"Predictable behavior: they don't show up."*

## 9. The through-line

Every reformulation died at the same joint, five times: **the mover.**

Statute needed a prosecutor. Criminal lane needed a prosecutor. Two-key trigger needed
an actor with authority. Judicial argument needed a court willing to unmake its own
doctrine. **Constitutional amendment needs a plaintiff with a cause of action, or an
executive willing to indict.**

He has never once failed to state what should be true. The instrument has failed five
times to name **who makes it true.** The amendment did not escape it — it moved the
vacancy from the charging decision to the courthouse door.

---

## 9a. Answers to the mover problem — third recursive pass

Scribe's analysis, not dictated. The through-line at §9 held that no version of the
instrument ever named **who makes it true.** Three answers exist that were raised by
neither the scribe nor any of the eight panel seats.

### A. The amendment supplies its own cause of action

The panel's most-agreed finding was that Section 1 **abolishes a defense without
creating a right to sue** — and that for federal officials no §1983 exists, *Bivens*
is nearly dead (*Egbert v. Boule*, 596 U.S. 482 (2022)), and courts refuse to imply
causes of action (*Alexander v. Sandoval*, 532 U.S. 275 (2001)).

**That is a drafting defect, not a structural one.** An amendment may create the right
directly rather than stripping a defense and hoping a remedy exists underneath. The
operative change is from *"Congress shall have power to enforce"* to language naming
the injured party as the mover:

```
Any person injured by a violation of this article may sue the official responsible
in any court of competent jurisdiction.
```

**Precedent that a self-executing amendment can have direct force:** the Thirteenth
operates without enabling legislation and reaches **private** conduct — *Civil Rights
Cases*, 109 U.S. 3 (1883); *Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co.*, 392 U.S. 409 (1968). It is
not true that a constitutional provision must wait on Congress.

**This is the single highest-value repair in the record.** It converts the mover from
an office that may decline into **the injured person**, who never declines.

### B. Qui tam — the tested private mover for a public claim

The two-key trigger died on Article II: a private underwriter cannot **command** the
executive to prosecute. But American law already contains a mechanism where a private
party **carries a government claim** — and it has survived for centuries.

The False Claims Act, 31 U.S.C. §3729 et seq., lets a private **relator** sue in the
government's stead and take a share of recovery. *Vermont Agency of Natural Resources
v. United States ex rel. Stevens*, 529 U.S. 765 (2000) upheld relator standing on the
theory that the relator is a **partial assignee of the government's claim**.

**The distinction that saves it is the one the two-key design missed: assignment, not
command.** The relator does not compel the executive to act. The relator acts *in the
executive's place*, and the executive retains the power to intervene or to move to
dismiss.

**Caveat, recorded:** qui tam is civil and is under live Article II pressure — Thomas,
J., dissenting in *United States ex rel. Polansky v. Executive Health Resources*, 599
U.S. 419 (2023), questioned its constitutionality, with concurring Justices noting the
question deserves consideration. It is standing but not unassailable.

### C. Reviewable declination — an answer to the omission gap

§4 left open that a prosecutor who **declines** to charge selects no one, exercises no
power, and trips neither face of the sword. The victim is harmed by the empty chair.

A mechanism that compels **reasons** rather than **charges** avoids Article II entirely,
because it regulates *process* and leaves the *decision* untouched: a declination in a
case confirmed by an independent auditor must be entered on the record with stated
grounds, published, and subject to review for pretext — with no power in the reviewer
to compel a charge.

**This does not force a prosecution. It ends silent non-enforcement**, which is the
actual mechanism by which official harm goes unanswered. *Heckler v. Chaney*, 470 U.S.
821 (1985) presumes enforcement discretion unreviewable; it does not hold that the
reasons for non-enforcement may be kept secret.

### What remains unanswered after this pass

**A and C together close the mover problem for the civil and the transparency lanes.
Neither closes it for criminal charging.** No mechanism found in this record compels an
indictment without colliding with Article II, and the amendment cannot manufacture a
prosecutor. **The criminal face of the sword still has no mover**, and that is now the
narrowest open question in the case.

## 9b. Closing the gaps — fourth recursive pass

Scribe's analysis. Three of the six open items close.

### Defining "un-jurisdictional" so *Stump* cannot be imported — CLOSES

§6 held that the offense's load-bearing term arrives pre-hollowed, because *Stump v.
Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978) read *"clear absence of all jurisdiction"* so narrowly
that a court of general jurisdiction almost never falls outside it.

**The defect is the direction of the test.** *Stump* asks whether the act was
**forbidden**. A court of general jurisdiction is rarely forbidden anything, so the
exception is empty by construction.

**Reverse it. Ask whether the act was authorized.**

```
An act is outside jurisdiction where no provision of constitution, statute, or
rule affirmatively authorizes the official to take it.
```

**The model already exists in administrative law**, where a body may act only within
what was delegated to it — *Louisiana Public Service Comm'n v. FCC*, 476 U.S. 355
(1986): *"an agency literally has no power to act… unless and until Congress confers
power upon it."*

**Applied to *Stump* itself, the case comes out the other way.** No Indiana statute
authorized a judge to order a fifteen-year-old sterilized on her mother's petition. Under
absence-of-prohibition he was immune. Under positive-authorization he acted outside
jurisdiction and the sword reaches him.

### The structural-cruelty gap — CLOSES at the moment of knowledge

The doctrine requires **"Willing act"**; the whitepaper holds structural cruelty needs no
awareness. The instrument therefore reached the cruel official and not the cruel system,
and immunity itself lives in the half it could not reach.

**The bridge is knowledge.** A structurally cruel practice becomes reflectively cruel
**for the official who learns it harms and continues operating it.** The system is never
a defendant; the official who knows and continues is. That is precisely the whitepaper's
own line — *"I know this hurts you. I will continue."*

**And the standard already exists in federal law:** deliberate indifference — actual
knowledge of the risk plus disregard of it — *Farmer v. Brennan*, 511 U.S. 825 (1994).

This does not criminalize a system. It criminalizes **continuing to run one after
learning what it does**, which is the only form in which a system is ever anybody's act.

### The line under self-propagation — ALREADY IN HIS TEXT

The scribe objected that budget-seeking and broad statutory readings are propagation and
also the job, and that the text supplied no line.

**The objection was wrong. The line is his phrasing.** Passage VII reads *"Willing act of
**sole purpose** of virus, self propegation."*

**Sole purpose is the limiter.** An official arguing an expansive reading in order to
*do the function* has a functional purpose and falls outside. An official acting to
entrench where no function is served has only the power as its own object. That is the
virus, and his words already drew the line the scribe asked for.

### What does not close

**The criminal mover.** Nothing in this record compels an indictment without colliding
with Article II. §9a(C) ends silent declination; it does not produce a prosecutor.

**The seal.** The doctrine remains unsealed. That is his word to give, not the scribe's.

## 9c. Attack on the repairs — fifth recursive pass

The third and fourth passes produced four repairs. This pass turns the panel's own
objections back onto them. **All four take damage. One is refuted by his own argument.**

### Against 9a(A) — the self-executing cause of action

**It solves the mover and worsens the flood.** Panel two's chair already lodged this
against the earlier draft: self-execution *"plausibly creates an unbounded, judicially
unprecedented federal cause of action"* — every official at every level, for every
alleged harm, filed under 28 U.S.C. §1331, with no exhaustion requirement, no damages
cap, and no limitations period, in front of the very federal judges now personally
exposed.

**The repair walks into an objection already on the record.** Naming the injured party
as the mover is the only thing that makes the article operate; it is also the thing that
makes its volume unbounded. **These are the same clause.** No version of the text found
so far separates them.

### Against 9a(B) — qui tam

**It reimports the defect that killed the insurer branch.** A relator's motive is a
share of the recovery. That is a direct pecuniary interest in the outcome of an
enforcement action — the precise infirmity condemned in *Tumey v. Ohio*, 273 U.S. 510
(1927) and measured under *Marshall v. Jerrico*, 446 U.S. 238 (1980), and the reason the
two-key trigger's underwriter branch failed.

Qui tam survives that objection only because *Vermont Agency* framed the relator as an
**assignee** rather than an adjudicator. Whether that framing holds when the defendant is
a **judge or a prosecutor** rather than a contractor is untested, and *Polansky* (2023)
shows the Article II foundation is being probed. **Same defect, new coat, weaker
footing.**

### Against 9b — the positive-authorization test — OVERBROAD

Reversing *Stump* fixes the emptiness of the exception and **overshoots**.

Officials constantly act without affirmative authorization for the specific act: a judge
setting a hearing time, an officer choosing a patrol route, a clerk sequencing a docket.
Under a strict positive-authorization test **nearly all official conduct becomes
un-jurisdictional**, and an offense that reaches everything reaches nothing — it collapses
back into prosecutorial selection, which is the mover problem again.

The test needs a limiting principle it does not yet have: it must attach to the act that
**produces the harm element**, not to every act in the chain. That limit is not drafted.

### Against 9b — the deliberate-indifference bridge — REFUTED BY HIS OWN ARGUMENT

This is the sharpest self-inflicted wound in the record.

The structural-cruelty gap was closed by requiring **knowledge**: the official who learns
the system harms and continues is willing. The standard borrowed was *Farmer v. Brennan*,
511 U.S. 825 (1994) — **subjective** deliberate indifference, requiring proof of actual
awareness.

**But the doctrine's own attack on qualified immunity is that knowledge cannot be
proven.** Passage II: *"you CANNOT hold any form of immunity from the law, due to
misconseptions of knowing the law better than thou. **because knowledge cant be proven**
for one."*

**The repair rebuilds the very epistemic defect the doctrine was written to destroy.** It
replaces "did the official know the law was clearly established" with "did the official
know the practice was harming," and both are unprovable mental states standing between a
victim and a remedy.

**Either the epistemic objection is sound and this bridge fails, or the bridge holds and
the objection in Passage II is weaker than stated. Both cannot survive.** This is now the
central unresolved contradiction in the case and it did not exist before the fourth pass
created it.
