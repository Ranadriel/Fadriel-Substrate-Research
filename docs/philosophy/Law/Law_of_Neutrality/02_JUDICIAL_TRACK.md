## I. Ranadriel's Position

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` No actor may hold an immunity they have not earned. A judge's title is a responsibility, not a right, and it does not by itself carry protection.

---

## II. What the Law Is Now

| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| *Bradley v. Fisher*, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 335 (1871) | Origin of absolute judicial immunity. Lost only for acts taken in the *clear absence of all jurisdiction*, not for acts merely in excess of it. |
| *Pierson v. Ray*, 386 U.S. 547 (1967) | The immunity applies to civil-rights suits under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. |
| *Stump v. Sparkman*, 435 U.S. 349 (1978) | Immunity covers any act that is a function normally performed by a judge, dealing with the judge in a judicial capacity — however wrong, however grave the harm. |
| *Pulliam v. Allen*, 466 U.S. 522 (1984) | Immunity does **not** bar prospective injunctive relief or attorney's fees against a state judge. |
| *Forrester v. White*, 484 U.S. 219 (1988) | Administrative acts (firing an employee) are not judicial acts; no absolute immunity. |
| *Mireles v. Waco*, 502 U.S. 9 (1991) (per curiam) | A judge alleged to have ordered officers to seize a public defender with excessive force and bring him into the courtroom remained absolutely immune. |
| Federal Courts Improvement Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-317, § 309 | Congress amended § 1983 so that injunctive relief against a judicial officer for judicial acts is barred unless a declaratory decree was violated or declaratory relief was unavailable — legislatively closing much of the door *Pulliam* opened. |

**The doctrine's own justification:** judges must be free to rule without fear of suit by losing parties, and a wronged party does not need to sue the judge because the error can be corrected **on appeal**.

---

## III. Exhibit A — *Stump v. Sparkman*

In 1971 an Indiana circuit judge approved a mother's petition to have her 15-year-old daughter sterilized. There was no hearing, no notice to the girl, and no one appointed to speak for her, and the petition was never filed as a case. She was told the operation was to remove her appendix. She learned the truth years later, after she married and could not conceive. The Supreme Court held the judge absolutely immune: approving petitions is the kind of thing judges do.

**Why it breaks the doctrine on its own terms.** The justification is appeal. Appeal presumes the injured party knows a ruling exists. She had no case number, no notice, and no knowledge that anything had been decided about her body. The one remedy the doctrine relies on was impossible by design. **The immunity outlived its own reason.**

The Court protected the *title*: a person who is a judge, doing a judge-like thing. The only defensible thing to protect is the *function*: a ruling made through actual process. Strip the process away and nothing remains but the title — and a title is a responsibility.

## IV. Proposed Platform — `[Proposed — Seed, 2026-10-22; awaiting Ranadriel]`

### Plank 1 — Protection follows a recorded, reviewable act, not the title.
A judge is immune only for acts **on a record** — docketed, written — and **open to review** by appeal, motion, or hearing. Remove either and the act is no longer judicial in any meaningful sense; the title does not protect it. *Stump* fails both: no docket, no case, no route to review.

*Revised 2026-10-22.* The first version also required **notice** to the affected party. It broke on the hardest question against it: warrants, wiretap orders, and emergency protective orders are issued without notice by design, and the first version would have stripped immunity from every judge who signs one ([HARD_QUESTIONS.md](./HARD_QUESTIONS.md), Q7). Ex parte orders are recorded and reviewable; *Stump* was neither.

### Plank 2 — The existing exceptions are too narrow to matter.
Courts deny immunity only for non-judicial acts (*Forrester*) or a clear absence of all jurisdiction (*Bradley*). *Mireles* shows how little that covers: an order to drag a lawyer into court by force was still a "judicial act."

### Plank 3 — The 1996 Act is a legislative choice, and the first target.
*Pulliam* let citizens stop an ongoing judicial violation by injunction. Congress closed that door by statute twelve years later. A statute Congress chose to pass is far easier to argue against than two centuries of common law, and repealing § 309 restores a remedy the Supreme Court itself recognized.

### Plank 4 — The same tiers as every other track.
- A wrong ruling made through real process is the judicial form of negligence: **appeal is the remedy**, no personal liability.
- A knowing or reckless act outside process — no record, no route to review — carries **personal liability**, and the state still pays the injured party in full.

(The tiers are defined once, in [04_REMEDY_ARCHITECTURE.md](./04_REMEDY_ARCHITECTURE.md).)

## V. Objections to Answer

| Objection | Direction of answer |
|---|---|
| Judicial independence: judges ruling in fear of suit | `[Proposed]` Process-bound immunity keeps full protection for every real ruling. Only acts stripped of process lose it, and those are not independence; they are its abuse. |
| A flood of suits from losing litigants | `[Proposed]` A losing litigant had a record and an appeal. Plank 1 leaves them where they are now: appeal. A docket sheet ends the suit at the pleading stage. |
| Who judges the judge? | `[Proposed]` Make the test documentary — docket, written order, route to review — so the facts can go to a jury. Still wounded: a judge rules on the motion to dismiss ([HARD_QUESTIONS.md](./HARD_QUESTIONS.md), Q6). |
| *Stump* is one extreme case | `[Open]` Needs more exhibits. Candidates to research: no-notice orders, ex parte orders affecting liberty, debtor's-prison and fines-and-fees courts, orders entered without jurisdiction over the person. |
