    Method

    The census was built in four passes, three of them through Green’s Dictionary of Slang, the largest historical dictionary of English slang. The full paper trail, including where each of Green’s results ended up, is in the census audit.

    1. The headword walk. Every Green’s entry links to the next one in alphabetical order. Starting at fuck, n., the survey followed those links one entry at a time to fuck you!, the last headword that begins with the word: all 73 entries, every numbered sense of each.

    2. The search sweep. Green’s search for fuck reports 277 results (entries, sub-entries and cross-references) but displays only 30 at a time. Splitting it by the letter that follows the word (fucka, fuckb … fuckz), each slice small enough to display in full and each checked against the count Green’s reported, listed all 277. Every one is accounted for: 250 counted as senses, 20 cross-references to entries already counted, 7 spelling variants folded in, none missing.

    3. The second-word sweep. Green’s search only matches the start of a headword, so words where fuck comes second (eyefuck, get fucked, Bumfuck, Egypt, absofuckinglutely) need their own searches. The survey searched each plausible first element (kin, body parts, animals, emotions, nationalities, phrase openers, the infixed words, every relative named in the 277) and opened every hit. Searches that found nothing are logged too.

    4. The disguises. Stand-ins recorded by other sources fill out the Fog of Euphemism.

    Each numbered sense counts as its own fuck: fuck up meaning “to ruin” and fuck up meaning “to hurt” are two sightings, because they are two meanings. Spelling variants of one sense are folded together, and pure cross-references aren’t counted twice. Glosses are written fresh for this atlas.

    Dates are the earliest citation each source shows, so they mark when a sense was first written down, not when it was first said. Where another source dates something earlier than Green’s (the Oxford English Dictionary’s 1503 fukkit, Etymonline’s c.1916 fuck up), the earlier date is used.

    The undated. 82 fucks are counted without a date. Green’s longest entries (fuck, n., fuck, v., and a few others such as cunt, n. and monkey, n.) run past what the survey’s tools can load, and the page text stops partway. What sits past the cut can still be found, because Green’s search index lists every sub-entry with its gloss, but its quotations, and so its first date, can’t be read. They appear on the plate only at the present day.

    So: is this all the fucks? Passes 1 and 2 are exhaustive: every Green’s headword that begins with the word is here, checked against Green’s own count. Pass 3 is exhaustive over every first element it tried, and the audit lists them all. The word itself hasn’t finished. Green’s logs new fucks from 2023, 2024 and 2025, so any census of it is a snapshot.
