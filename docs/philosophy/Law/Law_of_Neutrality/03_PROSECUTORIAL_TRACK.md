## I. Why This Track Exists

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-13]` Judicial and prosecutorial absolute immunity each need their own examination. Prosecutors are not judges: they choose whom to charge and what evidence to bring, and their protection rests on its own case law.

---

## II. Starting Points for Research

| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| *Imbler v. Pachtman*, 424 U.S. 409 (1976) | Absolute immunity for acts intimately associated with the judicial phase of the criminal process — initiating a prosecution and presenting the case. |
| *Buckley v. Fitzsimmons*, 509 U.S. 259 (1993) | Only qualified immunity for investigative work (acting like a detective) and for statements to the press. |
| *Van de Kamp v. Goldstein*, 555 U.S. 335 (2009) | Absolute immunity extends to supervision, training, and information-system management tied to trial conduct. |

The doctrine is already *functional*: it protects the act, not the office. That gives this track a foothold the judicial track had to argue for, because the line between protected advocacy and unprotected conduct is already drawn. The fight is over where it is drawn.
