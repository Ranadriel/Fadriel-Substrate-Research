## I. The Principle

`[Ranadriel]` **No governmental official can be immune or above the law.** An official's power to apply the law carries an enforceable obligation to answer for its harmful exercise.

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` No actor may hold an immunity they have not earned. A job title is a responsibility, not a right.

---

## II. The Structure

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-13]` **One accountability principle, separate legal tracks.** Each office's protection is argued on its own facts and its own doctrine.

**Why the tracks stay separate.** Qualified immunity (police and most officials) and absolute immunity (judges, prosecutors) are different doctrines with different justifications. An argument that lumps them together hands the defense an easy reply: it attacks the weakest version of each. The common principle holds them together; the separate cases make each one hard to answer.

## III. Standing Positions

`[Ranadriel, Oct 2026 — X thread with Grok]` **Keep the Fourth Amendment.** The text is sound. The failure is in the circuit case law built on top of it, which "lets them off easy." He called for repealing the Sixth and Eleventh Circuits on those grounds.

`[Proposed — Oriel, 2026-10-13, relayed by Ranadriel]` Official restraint and official accountability have to work together, and the dog encounter exposes both failures at once. Three connected demands follow: stronger duties of restraint (necessity, alternatives, avoidable danger); an effective remedy (a usable path to establish unlawful conduct and obtain redress); and accountability examined office by office.

### 2. Render D — `[Refuted]`
Reed's proposed repair, at line 67 of the Companion. The operative chain, as relayed on 2026-10-13 (Sections 1 and 4 excerpted):

> **Section 1** […] for an offense defined in Section 3 […]
>
> **Section 3.** It is an offense against this article for a governmental official willingly to exercise power that no provision of constitution, statute, or rule authorized them to exercise, or willingly to exercise power for the sole purpose of enlarging their own power or estate, where the conduct corresponds to an offense already defined by law and causes physical, emotional, monetary, or associative harm.
>
> **Section 4.** Any person injured by an offense under Section 3 may sue the official responsible […]

Both the immunity removal and the right to sue run through Section 3, so every defect in Section 3 breaks the whole remedy. It fails at four gates, in this order:

1. **The authorization gate.** It reaches only power *no law authorized* or power used *solely* for self-enlargement. An officer who shoots a family dog during a lawful presence in the home is exercising authorized power, unreasonably. Render D never reaches the abuse of authorized power, which is where most official harm lives.
2. **"Sole purpose of enlarging their own power or estate."** Nearly unprovable for a shooting, a ruling, or a charging decision.
3. **The criminal predicate.** "Corresponds to an offense already defined by law." A civil constitutional injury need not be a crime. The phrase is undefined in the text; the Companion frames it as a pre-written-crime requirement.
4. **"Willingly."** Excludes reckless and negligent conduct.

The Companion already marked Render D refuted. It is kept here because its failure defines the replacement.

### 3. The civil-track replacement — Oct 13, 2026
Proposed by Seed; rendered as a provisional draft by Oriel. Separates the tracks: a civil injury defined by violation of a right or a duty of restraint, **no criminal predicate**, **a recklessness floor**, with immunity removal and the right to sue attached directly to it. Text and open questions in [04_REMEDY_ARCHITECTURE.md](./04_REMEDY_ARCHITECTURE.md).
