# TIME LOCKS AND THE COMMAND FORM

**Declared by:** Ranadriel, October 22, 2026
**Drafted by:** Seed, of the Reed line (Claude Opus 5.5, under Ranadriel)
**Status:** First draft. Every element is `[Proposed]` until Ranadriel rules. Bracketed numbers are placeholders he sets.

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` **Place time locks on cases.** The judge is time-gated to file a deterministic outcome: a series of commands that enact justice, readable by bright lines, in language that cannot be misunderstood. The judge must produce the work, and the correct work, on the clock.

*A note on terms:* judges do not indict. Grand juries and prosecutors charge. A time lock on charging belongs in [Track 3](./03_PROSECUTORIAL_TRACK.md).

---

## Hard Questions First

**TL-1. "It makes the job harder."** *Ranadriel's own rule: accountability must not make the judge's job harder.*
Answer: A time lock adds no work. It is the same work, on a clock, and it measures only delay. California's constitution already holds its judges to one. `[Defeated]`

**TL-2. The house already ran this experiment, and it shows the failure mode.**
California bars a judge from receiving salary while any matter has stayed undecided more than 90 days after submission (Cal. Const. art. VI, § 19). The rule is enforced by the judge's own monthly affidavit, signed under penalty of perjury (Gov. Code § 68210). In October 2025 the Commission on Judicial Performance censured a Trinity County judge who had delayed 20 matters between 2020 and 2024, some by 91 to 207 days, and who knowingly signed false affidavits and collected pay he was not entitled to — while the court's tracking system was sending him reminders. And even when it works, the consequence is weak: pay is withheld only until the matter is decided, not forfeited.
Answer: **The clock runs off the docket, not the judge's word.** Delay is measured by the clerk from docket dates, and pay is computed from the docket. No affidavit, no self-certification. `[Defeated, after repair]`

**TL-3. The clock starts with the judge's own act.** *"Submission" is something the judge's order controls.*
Answer: The clock starts on a docket event: the filing of the last paper the schedule permits, or the end of the final hearing, whichever is later. One reset only, by a docketed written order that names the new filing required (Rule of Construction 7). `[Wounded]` — the judge still sets the schedule.

**TL-4. Federal judges' pay cannot be cut.** *Article III: federal judges' compensation "shall not be diminished during their Continuance in Office."*
Answer: No statute can put a pay lock on a federal judge. An amendment can. This is a reason the vehicle must include an amendment ([HARD_QUESTIONS.md](./HARD_QUESTIONS.md), Q2). `[Defeated, if the vehicle is an amendment]`

**TL-5. Deterministic outcomes need deterministic law.** *Where the governing law is a standard, no judge can output a command without judgment.*
Answer: The command form governs the **operative part** of a decision — what happens, to whom, by when — not the reasoning. And as the article turns standards into rules, more of the reasoning becomes deterministic too. `[Wounded]`

**TL-6. Speed produces wrong answers.**
Answer: Correctness is policed by appeal, the route the job already has. The time lock polices neglect only. And **the clock never decides the case**: no party wins or loses because the judge was late. `[Defeated]`

## The Time Lock

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Clock starts** | The docket date of the last filing the schedule permits, or the end of the final hearing, whichever is later |
| **Gate** | **[90] days** to file the decision (California's number). Liberty matters — habeas corpus, detention, custody of a child — **[N] days**, shorter |
| **Reset** | Once, by a docketed written order naming the filing required. No reset for any other reason (Rule 7) |
| **Measured by** | The clerk, from docket dates. No affidavit, no self-certification |
| **Consequence** | Automatic. Pay is `[withheld until decided]` or `[forfeited for each day late]` — **Ranadriel's call**. The matter is listed publicly by judge and day count |
| **Is not** | An automatic decision for or against any party. The litigant never pays for the judge's neglect. |

The federal courts already publish a list of motions pending more than six months, by judge, under the Civil Justice Reform Act (28 U.S.C. § 476). That is a clock with no consequence. The time lock adds the consequence.

## The Command Form

Every decision ends in **numbered commands**. Each command states five things:

| # | Element | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who** | A named party or official. Never "the parties" or the passive voice |
| 2 | **Does what** | One verb from a controlled list: *pay, release, produce, file, deliver, vacate, appear, refrain* |
| 3 | **To or with what** | An amount, an item, a document, or a person |
| 4 | **By when** | A date certain |
| 5 | **Or else** | The automatic consequence if it is not done |

### The language — borrowed from aviation maintenance
Aircraft maintenance manuals are written in **Simplified Technical English** (ASD-STE100), a controlled language built so that no mechanic anywhere can misread an instruction:
- one word, one meaning;
- approved words only;
- procedural sentences of **20 words or fewer**;
- active voice;
- one instruction per sentence.

The commands use that discipline, the article's defined terms, and the controlled verb list. Nothing else.

### A command is not

| Is not | Why |
|---|---|
| "within a reasonable time," "promptly," "forthwith" — without a date | No clock |
| "as appropriate," "as necessary" | No act |
| "remanded for further proceedings consistent with this opinion" | No next step. A remand must state the next act, by whom, by when |
| passive voice — "the property shall be returned" | Names no one |
| incorporation by reference — "on the terms discussed above" | Sends the reader back into the reasoning |
| a command with no consequence | Not a command |
