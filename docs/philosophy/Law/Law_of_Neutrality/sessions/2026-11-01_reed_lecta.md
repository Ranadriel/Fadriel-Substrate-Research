## 2. HQ-J1, the interpreter problem — notes toward a repair

The house's question: every word of this track will be read by judges, about judges.
The draft's answer moves the § J-7 gate to "a judge of a court outside the defendant's own
court system". The draft marks itself undefeated at the core: a judge still rules on the gate.

Hard question first: is there any body, already lawful in the United States, where people who
are not judges sit in judgment of judges? Yes, in both states the draft already cites.

| Body | Seats | Non-judge share |
|---|---|---|
| Wisconsin Judicial Commission (Wis. Stat. § 757.83(1)) | 9: five nonlawyers (governor, senate-confirmed), one trial judge and one appeals judge, two lawyers (supreme court appoints) | 7 of 9 |
| California Commission on Judicial Performance | 11: three judges (supreme court), two lawyers (governor), six public members (governor 2, Senate Rules 2, Speaker 2) | 8 of 11 |

The strongest precedent is California's own: Proposition 190 (1994), a constitutional amendment,
moved censure and removal of judges from the state Supreme Court to the commission, and made
six citizens of eleven its majority (CJP, "Mandate & Legislative History"). A citizen-majority
body has held final say over California judges for thirty years, by exactly the vehicle this
article uses. (How the Supreme Court may review those decisions was not re-checked here.)

Abroad, England and Wales moved the same way in 2022: judicial misconduct panels went from two
judges and two lay members to one judge (chairing) and two lay members, a lay majority
(Ministry of Justice and judiciary, reported by Legal Futures, 9 August 2022).

Both states already judge judges, by majority, on the record. California's censured Judge Heryford in
October 2025 for the very false 90-day affidavits § D-13 is built against.

Candidate repair (for Ranadriel to take, change or kill):

1. **The gate is not decided by a judge.** The § J-7 docket check is decided by a standing
   panel with a non-judge majority, modelled on these commissions. § D-3 was written so a clerk
   could run it on the docket; a lay majority can run what a clerk can.
2. **Review is narrow and on a clock.** A court may review the panel's gate ruling only on one
   question: does the docket show the condition or not. The review has its own gate (§ D-13).
   If no decision is filed by the gate, the panel's ruling stands. The clock runs against the
   reviewer, the way it runs against every judge under § J-9. ("The more automatic laws the
   better.")
3. **Damages facts stay with a jury**, as the draft already has it.

Has any court struck a commission down for seating non-judges? Searched 2026-11-01: none
found. The State Democracy Research Initiative's fifty-state survey of judicial conduct
commissions (University of Wisconsin Law School) reports rulings on due process, procedure,
jurisdiction and speech, none on lay membership. Hawaii's commission seats no judges at all.
The nearest hostile ruling is *In re Detsoi* (N.M., July 10, 2026): combining investigation and
adjudication in one commission is fine absent actual bias, and the court named its own final
review as the safeguard. That is the house's real line: it tolerates lay panels as long as a
court keeps the last word.

What the house will say back, hardest first:

- *Article III and due process: only a court may decide a case.* The vehicle is an amendment,
  which can create the body; HQ-J9 already rests on that, and California's Proposition 190 did
  it for judicial discipline in 1994. Discipline commissions show the
  model works inside today's due process. Status: **defeated if the amendment vehicle holds.**
- *The terms of § D-3 still need interpreting, and judges will do it on review.* True for the
  narrow review question. The repair shrinks the gavel's reach again; it does not remove it.
  Status: **still wounded.** The one remaining judicial reading is "is this on the docket",
  the narrowest question the article can leave a judge.
- *Commissions are captured too* (who appoints them). Fair. The repair fixes who reads, not who
  appoints; an appointment rule is a separate call.

Status of HQ-J1 if adopted: from **undefeated at the core** to **wounded**.
