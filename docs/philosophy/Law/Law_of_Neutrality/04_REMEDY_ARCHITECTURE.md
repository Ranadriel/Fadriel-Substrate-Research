## I. The Civil Injury Track

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-13]` **Structure:** a civil injury with **no criminal predicate** and **a recklessness floor**. Immunity removal and the right to sue attach to the civil injury directly, without requiring criminal proceedings.

**Provisional text** — proposed by Seed, rendered by Oriel, 2026-10-13. Not yet ratified:

> A law-enforcement official acting under color of governmental authority shall be liable for injury caused by conduct that violates a right secured by this Constitution or a duty of restraint imposed by this article or by law, where the official acts intentionally or recklessly. Liability does not depend on the conduct constituting a criminal offense, the absence of general authority to exercise the power involved, or a purpose of personal gain.

Its last sentence closes, one by one, the gates where Render D failed (see [00_PRINCIPLE.md](./00_PRINCIPLE.md), §IV.2). Separate clauses in the Oriel draft attach immunity removal and the right to sue; a restraint clause covers conduct before the final act. Those clauses are in the local draft file and not yet imported.

**Scope note:** the text names law-enforcement officials only. Judges and prosecutors are handled in their own tracks.

## II. The Mental-State Tiers

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` **There is a bright line between recklessness and an accident.**

The line is **awareness at the moment of acting** — what the official knew, not what happened afterward. The Model Penal Code already states it precisely (§ 2.02(2)(c)–(d)).

| Tier | Test | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Accident** | Harm no one could reasonably have foreseen | No fault. The state compensates the injured party. |
| **Negligence** | Failed to see a substantial risk they should have seen | `[Proposed]` Covered, and forgivable through the self-report channel (§III). Personal liability lifted; the injured party is still paid by the state. |
| **Recklessness** | Was aware of a substantial risk and acted anyway | Liable. `[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` **Repentant recklessness is judged individually** — coming forward weighs as mitigation, not as automatic immunity. |
| **Intent** | Meant the act, knowing the facts that made it a violation | `[Proposed]` Liable. No self-report shield. |

**The object of recklessness** — `[Proposed — Seed, 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-22]`
Recklessness attaches to **the risk and the facts**, not to the law. The official knew the circumstances (dogs inside, children present, the owner beside the animal, alternatives available) and acted anyway. Whether those facts amount to a violation is judged objectively. No one has to prove the official *knew it was unlawful* — which would rebuild the very knowledge barrier the amendment exists to tear down.

**Unresolved tension** — `[Open]`
The 2026-10-13 structure sets a **recklessness floor** (negligence not liable). The 2026-10-22 proposal **covers negligence**, routed through self-report. Both cannot stand as written. Ranadriel decides whether the floor drops to negligence.

## III. The Self-Report Channel

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]`
> "An honest mistake granted and self reported should be granted immunity if admitted to the mistake. Mistakes are eternal, repented mistakes can be reversed before the damage gets out of hand."

**Design** — `[Proposed — Seed, 2026-10-22]`
1. **The shield covers the official, not the injury.** Self-report lifts *personal* liability and punishment. The injured party's remedy stays whole; the state pays.
2. **The report must come before discovery.** Once the footage surfaces or a complaint is filed, admission is no longer repentance; it is damage control, and the shield is off.

**Forced discovery forfeits it** — `[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]`
If the official has wronged someone badly enough that the civilian, or those helping them, have to force discovery in its earliest stages, the self-report protection is gone — and one must truly question the validity of the offense.

**The model it borrows** — `[Adopted, 2026-10-22]`
The NASA Aviation Safety Reporting System. A pilot who reports their own inadvertent violation promptly is generally spared FAA penalties, though the violation can still be found. Aviation buys honesty with protection, and the resulting data stream is a large part of why commercial flight became safe. Qualified immunity gives the shield away and buys nothing.

## IV. The Evidence Duty

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` **There should be legal fines and damages for any state official who fails to take evidence and process it expeditiously.**

**Mechanism** — `[Proposed — Seed, 2026-10-22]`
The law already has the tool: **spoliation.** When a litigant destroys, hides, or sits on evidence, a court can sanction them and instruct the jury to presume the missing evidence would have hurt them. Today that remedy is late and discretionary. The amendment moves it upstream and makes it automatic:
- an expedited processing timeline is a duty;
- missing the timeline is itself the offense, with fines attached;
- from that moment, the presumption runs against the state.

## V. How the Pieces Lock

| Official's choice | Result |
|---|---|
| Comes forward promptly | Protected personally (negligence) or mitigated (recklessness). Victim paid. |
| Waits until forced | Protection forfeit. Conduct presumed suspect. |
| Stalls on evidence in hand | New liability for the delay. Presumption against the state. |

**Delay is never the cheaper option for anyone in the chain.**
