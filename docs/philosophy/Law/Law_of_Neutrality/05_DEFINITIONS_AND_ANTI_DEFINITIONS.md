**Declared by:** Ranadriel, October 22, 2026
**Drafted by:** Seed, of the Reed line (Claude Opus 5.5, under Ranadriel)
**Status:** Second draft, full bore. Every clause is `[Proposed]` until Ranadriel rules. Bracketed numbers are placeholders he sets.
**Built from:** [STEP_1_STRIP_THE_SMOKE.md](./STEP_1_STRIP_THE_SMOKE.md)
**Reconcile with:** the definitions in `Law_of_Neutrality.md` (home machine) when it is imported.

> "We know what something can't be, so that defines what it can be."
> — Ranadriel, October 22, 2026

`[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` The article carries its own acts and definitions, and **anti-definitions: what each term is *not*.** The method is Cartesian. Doubt every reading of a term that the house has ever used, and reject each one in writing. What survives the doubt is the meaning.

Every term has two parts. **Means** says what it is. **Is not** closes, one by one, the doors the house has already used to stretch or shrink it. Each closed door names the case or doctrine that opened it. The catalog is experiential: it is built from what the house has actually done.

---

## Hard Questions First

**HQ-A. Expressio unius.** *A list of what a term is not invites the court to infer that anything unlisted is allowed.*
Answer: Rule of Construction 3 forbids any inference from omission. `[Wounded]` — the rule itself must survive interpretation.

**HQ-B. Renaming.** *Exclude a doctrine by name and the house renames it.* It already has, three times: "good faith and probable cause" (*Pierson v. Ray*, 1967); "objective legal reasonableness" measured against "clearly established" law (*Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, 1982); a right's contours placed "beyond debate" (*Ashcroft v. al-Kidd*, 2011). Same shield, new names.
Answer: **Anti-define by function, not by name.** "Immunity" is defined by what a rule *does*, "however named" (§ 1.12 and Rule 2). A renamed shield is still caught. `[Defeated, if § 1.12 holds]`

**HQ-C. Breadth.** *"No official" reaches legislators, whose speech and debate the Constitution itself protects (Art. I, § 6).*
Answer: `[Open]` — the article either amends that protection for injury claims or carves legislators out. A new front either way.

**HQ-D. Infinity.** *The space of what a thing is not is infinite. A finite list of "is nots" leaves the rest undefined, and the court fills the rest.*
Answer: **Negate the escapes, not the universe.** The house's escape routes are finite and documented: every one of them is a case in the reporters. Each "is not" below cites the escape it closes. New escapes are caught by function (Rule 2) and by the bar on inference from omission (Rule 3). `[Wounded]` — the house will invent new routes; the functional definitions must be wide enough to meet them.

**HQ-E. Prolixity.** *Chief Justice Marshall: a constitution that spelled out every detail "would partake of the prolixity of a legal code." Only its "great outlines" should be marked (McCulloch v. Maryland, 1819). A catalog of exclusions is a code, not a constitution.*
Answer: The "great outlines" doctrine is the license that hands the interpreter the gavel. The article is long exactly where the house has been inventive, and nowhere else. Most state constitutions already run many times longer than the federal one. `[Wounded]` — **decision for Ranadriel:** carry the full catalog in the article itself, in a schedule attached to it, or in an implementing statute the article protects from narrowing.

**HQ-F. The bribed judge on the record.** *In the Luzerne County "kids for cash" litigation, a federal court held two judges who took payments tied to a for-profit juvenile detention scheme immune for their in-court adjudications — including rulings sending children to detention without counsel. Only the claims over the out-of-court scheme could proceed (M.D. Pa., 2009). Those rulings were docketed, written, and appealable. The first draft of § 1.13 would have protected them too.*
Answer: § 1.13 now strips protection from any act where the judge received anything of value from a person with an interest in the outcome, and requires that the route to review be given **in writing** to the person affected. `[Defeated, after repair]`

## Section 1 — Definitions

### § 1.1 "Official"
**Means** every person who exercises any power of the United States, of a State, or of any political subdivision of either, whether by election, appointment, employment, or contract.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| limited to employees | The state-action gap for contractors performing government functions |
| narrowed by title, rank, or office | Title-based shields: *Bradley*, *Stump*, *Imbler* |
| limited to state and local officials | Section 1983 reaches only state actors; federal officers answer only through a court-implied remedy (§ 1.15) |
| suspended off duty, when acting under color of office | The "personal pursuits" exclusion (*Screws v. United States*, 1945, plurality) |
| changed by the capacity in which the official is named in a suit | Official-capacity damages suits against state officers are not suits against "persons" (*Will v. Michigan Dep't of State Police*, 1989) |
| excused while testifying | Absolute witness immunity, including for police perjury at trial (*Briscoe v. LaHue*, 1983) and before a grand jury (*Rehberg v. Paulk*, 2012) |
| excused because the function is "quasi-judicial" | Absolute immunity extended to agency adjudicators and prosecutors who are not judges (*Butz v. Economou*, 1978) |
| `[Open]` a legislator acting legislatively | *Tenney v. Brandhove* (1951); *Bogan v. Scott-Harris* (1998); Speech or Debate (HQ-C) |

### § 1.2 "Law-enforcement official"
**Means** any official who holds a power of arrest, or who carries a weapon by government authority.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| narrowed to exclude contractors, reserve or auxiliary officers, or campus, transit, school, or corrections officers | Definitional carve-outs by employer type |
| narrowed by the jurisdiction the official was outside of when acting | "Outside jurisdiction, therefore private citizen" defenses |

### § 1.3 "Under color of office"
**Means** acting while on duty, or using a power, badge, uniform, weapon, vehicle, record, or access that the person holds only by reason of office.

| Is not conditioned on | Closes |
|---|---|
| the act being authorized by any law | Render D's authorization gate. The house itself rejected this escape in *Monroe v. Pape* (1961); the article fixes that holding in text so it cannot be reopened. |
| the official's motive being official | The "personal pursuits" exclusion (*Screws*) |
| the act being within the scope of employment | Scope-of-employment tests in state tort law and federal scope certification (Westfall Act, 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d)) |
| the act benefiting the employing government | Benefit-to-employer tests |

### § 1.4 "Violation"
**Means** an act or omission of an official, under color of office, that
- (a) breaches a duty stated in this article; or
- (b) deprives a person of a right secured by this Constitution.

| Is not conditioned on | Closes |
|---|---|
| a finding that the act was unreasonable, for path (a) | The reasonableness standard (*Graham v. Connor*, 1989) |
| any court having previously held similar conduct unlawful | "Clearly established" (*Harlow*; *Pearson v. Callahan*, 2009) |
| the official knowing the conduct was unlawful | Willfulness as specific intent to deprive a right (*Screws*) |
| the conduct being a crime, or on any prosecution or conviction | Render D's criminal predicate |
| a "seizure" having occurred | The intent-to-seize requirement (*Brower v. County of Inyo*, 1989), under which hostages shot by police were held not "seized" (*Childress v. City of Arapaho*, 10th Cir. 2000) |
| the conduct "shocking the conscience" | The substantive due process standard (*County of Sacramento v. Lewis*, 1998) |

| Is not excused by | Closes |
|---|---|
| a danger the official's own violation created | The Court's rejection of the "provocation rule" (*County of Los Angeles v. Mendez*, 2017), and the question *Barnes v. Felix* (2025) left open |
| the speed of the decision | "Tense, uncertain, and rapidly evolving" deference (*Graham*) |
| the official's perception, where a recording shows otherwise | "The perspective of a reasonable officer on the scene" (*Graham*), measured instead against the record |
| the violation being negligent | "Mere negligence" is not a due process deprivation (*Daniels v. Williams*, 1986) |
| `[Open]` being an omission, where the article states a duty to act | No constitutional duty to protect (*DeShaney v. Winnebago County*, 1989; *Castle Rock v. Gonzales*, 2005) |

### § 1.5 "Injury"
**Means** any of the following, caused by a violation or by an act under color of office:
- death;
- bodily injury;
- loss of liberty;
- loss of, damage to, or destruction of property, **including an animal**;
- `[Open]` emotional, monetary, and associative harm, as Render D listed them — each to be defined if retained.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| limited to physical harm | The physical-injury requirement for prisoners' emotional-harm claims (42 U.S.C. § 1997e(e)) |
| subject to any minimum degree | "De minimis" thresholds (cf. *Wilkins v. Gaddy*, 2010) |
| conditioned on the conduct being a crime | Render D |
| reduced to nominal damages when hard to measure | Nominal damages for unquantified injury (*Carey v. Piphus*, 1978). Replace with a fixed minimum of **[N]** per violation. |

### § 1.6 "Animal"
**Means** any animal kept by a person.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| valued as property alone; damages are not limited to market value | The market-value rule for animals in state law, and dogs treated only as "effects" in Fourth Amendment cases |

### § 1.7 "Knowledge"
**Means** an official knows a fact when
- (a) they are aware of it; or
- (b) **deemed knowledge:** the fact was stated in their presence and is audible or visible on their own recording device, or on any recording in their employer's custody.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| proof of what the official believed about the law | Belief-based defenses (good faith; *Pierson*) |
| defeated by the official's statement that they did not hear or see what the recording captured | Credibility contests over warnings |
| defeated by a missing recording; a recording the official failed to make or keep raises the adverse presumption of § 1.11 | Non-activation and lost-footage defenses |
| deemed for a fact not audible or visible on the recording | (fairness limit) |
| `[Open]` deemed for a statement in a language the official does not speak | (fairness limit) |
| `[Open]` limited to the official who heard it | **Inversion:** the house lets officers act on what any of them knows (the collective-knowledge doctrine, *United States v. Hensley*, 1985). The article could make a recorded fact known to every official present. |

### § 1.8 The four states of mind

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| **Accident** | An injury caused under color of office that follows from **no violation**. |
| **Negligence** | A violation committed **without knowledge** of the facts that make it a violation. |
| **Recklessness** | A violation committed **with knowledge** of the facts that make it a violation. |
| **Intent** | A violation committed **for the purpose** of causing the injury. |

*No degree words ("substantial," "unjustifiable," "gross") appear in these definitions. That is deliberate.*

**Accident is not:**

| Is not an accident because | Closes |
|---|---|
| the official did not intend the injury | Intent-based due process thresholds (*Daniels*; *Lewis*) |
| the official did not foresee it | Foreseeability as an excuse |
| the official acted quickly | "Split-second" deference (*Graham*) |
| the official was mistaken about the law | Reasonable mistake of law (*Heien v. North Carolina*, 2014) |
| the official was mistaken about a fact on the recording | Deemed knowledge (§ 1.7) |
| the official's own violation created the danger | *Mendez* (2017) |
| the person injured was not the intended target | *Brower*; *Childress* |

**Negligence is not** excused by workload, haste, or the official's training.

**Recklessness is not** conditioned on "deliberate indifference" (*Farmer v. Brennan*, 1994), on conduct that "shocks the conscience" (*Lewis*), on knowing the act was unlawful, or on intending the injury.

**Intent is not** conditioned on intent to deprive a right (*Screws*), and is not excused by any belief that the injury was deserved.

### § 1.9 "Self-report"
**Means** a written report by the official that
- names the date, place, and persons affected, and the official's own act;
- is delivered to `[the designated office]`, independent of the official's chain of command, within **[10] days** of the act; and
- is delivered **before any trigger event** (§ 1.10).

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| a report delivered after a trigger event | Damage control posing as repentance |
| a report made for the official by another person | Reports drafted by counsel or a representative |
| a report that omits the official's own act | Vague, passive-voice reports |
| a use-of-force report required by an employer, unless it meets every element above | Routine paperwork posing as self-report |
| a report delivered only within the official's own chain of command | Reports buried in-house |
| available for an act that is a crime | (aviation's criminal carve-out) |
| available more than **[once in N years]** | Filing as routine insurance |
| `[Open]` a report filed after the official has reviewed recordings of the incident | Contract provisions that delay statements until footage is reviewed |

### § 1.10 "Trigger event"
**Means** the earliest of:
- the filing of a complaint, claim, or notice of claim naming the incident;
- a records request naming the incident;
- public release of any recording of the incident;
- notice to the official of any investigation of the incident.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| `[Open]` delayed by the official's lack of knowledge of the event | Claimed ignorance of the complaint |

### § 1.11 "Evidence deadline"
**Means**
- a recording is preserved within **[24 hours]** of the incident;
- physical evidence is logged within **[24 hours]**;
- a recording is produced to the injured person or their counsel within **[30] days** of a written request.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| extended by workload or backlog | Backlog excuses |
| satisfied by deleting a recording under a retention schedule while any trigger event is pending, or within **[N] years** | Automatic deletion |
| satisfied where the device was never activated; non-activation counts as a missing recording | Discretionary activation policies |
| conditioned on payment of fees | Records fees |
| extended by redaction | Redaction delays |
| `[Open]` extended by the pendency of any investigation | The investigation exemption in records laws |

**Charged to:** the employing government for systemic delay; the official personally only where the evidence was in their custody and the deadline passed without action.

### § 1.12 "Immunity"
**Means** any rule, **however named**, that bars, defeats, delays, or limits liability or suit under this article because of
- (a) the official's title, office, or role;
- (b) the official's belief in the lawfulness of the act;
- (c) the absence of a prior decision addressing similar facts;
- (d) the sovereign character of the employing government; or
- (e) any doctrine of the common law.

**Includes by name** — the catalog of shields:

| Ground | Shield | Source |
|---|---|---|
| (a) title or role | Absolute judicial immunity, except for a judicial act (§ 1.13) | *Bradley v. Fisher* (1871); *Stump* (1978); *Mireles v. Waco* (1991) |
| (a) | Quasi-judicial immunity | *Butz v. Economou* (1978) |
| (a) | Witness immunity for officials | *Briscoe* (1983); *Rehberg* (2012) |
| (a) | `[Open]` Absolute prosecutorial immunity | *Imbler v. Pachtman* (1976); *Van de Kamp v. Goldstein* (2009) |
| (a) | `[Open]` Legislative immunity | *Tenney*; *Bogan* |
| (b) belief | The good-faith defense | *Pierson* (1967) |
| (c) no prior case | Qualified immunity | *Harlow*; *Pearson*; *al-Kidd* |
| (d) sovereignty | Sovereign immunity, in any court | *Hans v. Louisiana* (1890); *Alden v. Maine* (1999); *Seminole Tribe v. Florida* (1996) |
| (d) | "Not a person" | *Will* (1989) |
| (d) | The discretionary-function exception | Federal Tort Claims Act, 28 U.S.C. § 2680(a) |
| (d) | `[Open]` The bar on suits by servicemembers | *Feres v. United States* (1950) |
| (e) common law | The public-duty doctrine | State tort law |
| procedure, functioning as a shield | Limits on court-implied remedies | *Ziglar v. Abbasi* (2017); *Egbert v. Boule* (2022) — see § 1.15 |
| procedure | Substitution of the government for a federal employee in tort suits | Westfall Act, 28 U.S.C. § 2679(d) |

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| the protection this article itself grants: the self-report protection (§ 1.9) and the judicial-act protection (§ 1.13) | (the only two shields left standing) |

### § 1.13 "Judicial act"
**Means** an act of a judge that
- (a) bears a docket number assigned at or before the act;
- (b) is in writing or on a transcribed record; and
- (c) is reviewable by appeal, by motion, or at a hearing set; **and, for an order taking a person's liberty, bodily integrity, custody of a child, or property, the route to review was given in writing to the person affected.**

*Scope note:* `[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` accountability must not make the judge's job harder. Element (c)'s written-notice condition therefore reaches only orders against liberty, body, a child, or property, where written notice of appeal rights is already standard practice at criminal sentencing.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| an act missing any of the three | *Stump*: no docket, no case, no route to review |
| an administrative or employment act | *Forrester v. White* (1988) — kept |
| judicial because it is of a kind normally performed by a judge | *Stump* |
| judicial because the judge had general jurisdiction over the subject | *Bradley*; *Stump* |
| judicial because it was done in a courtroom or from the bench | *Mireles* |
| protected where the judge received anything of value from any person with an interest in the outcome, or was convicted of an offense involving the act | Immunity "even when the judge is accused of acting maliciously and corruptly" (*Pierson*; *Bradley*); the "kids for cash" rulings (HQ-F) |
| an act of a person who is not a judge | Quasi-judicial extension (*Butz*) |
| `[Open]` protected where the record shows the judge refused or failed to appoint counsel that the law required | The "kids for cash" adjudications without counsel. Keyed to the judge's own act on the record, not to public-defender funding, so the judge answers for neglect and never for a budget. |

### § 1.14 "Employing government"
**Means** the United States, a State, or a political subdivision of either, on whose behalf the official acted.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| relieved of liability for want of a policy or custom | *Monell v. Dep't of Social Services* (1978) |
| relieved because no "deliberate indifference" in training is shown | *City of Canton v. Harris* (1989); *Connick v. Thompson* (2011) |
| protected by sovereign immunity of any kind | *Hans*; *Alden*; *Seminole Tribe* |
| excluded as not a "person" | *Will* |
| relieved by the discretionary-function exception | 28 U.S.C. § 2680(a) |
| relieved because the duty ran to the public at large | The public-duty doctrine |
| protected by any notice-of-claim requirement shorter than **[N] days** | State notice-of-claim statutes |
| `[Open]` protected by any damages cap below **[N]** | State tort-claims caps; New Mexico's Civil Rights Act cap |

### § 1.15 "Remedy" — *new*
**Means** the right of a person injured by a violation to sue the official and the employing government, in any state or federal court of competent jurisdiction, **created by this article itself.**

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| conditioned on any court implying a cause of action | *Bivens* limits: *Ziglar v. Abbasi* (2017); *Egbert v. Boule* (2022) |
| subject to a limitation period shorter than **[N] years**, running from the day the injured person discovers the violation | Borrowed state personal-injury periods (*Owens v. Okure*, 1989; *Wallace v. Kato*, 2007) |
| limited to nominal damages for a proven violation | *Carey* (1978) |
| conditioned on physical injury | 42 U.S.C. § 1997e(e) |
| conditioned on exhausting any administrative remedy | Prison exhaustion rules (42 U.S.C. § 1997e(a)) |
| `[Open]` barred by a conviction arising from the same events | The favorable-termination bar (*Heck v. Humphrey*, 1994) |

### § 1.16 "Attack" — *new; for lethal force against an animal*
**Means** `[Proposed — Seed, 2026-10-22]` the animal's bite **breaks a person's skin**, or the animal **holds its grip** on a person. Both are physical facts that a recording, a wound, or a medical record proves.

*Why a physical line:* `[Ranadriel, 2026-10-22]` a bite at that moment "could be equally play." Play and attack cannot be told apart by guessing an animal's intent, so the article does not ask anyone to guess. Until skin breaks or the grip holds, there is no attack, and an officer — who accepted the risk of a bite on entering armed — has no ground for lethal force.

**Deemed attack** `[Proposed]`: an animal is attacking, whether or not it has bitten, when a person present gives it an attack command that is audible on a recording. The command turns the animal into a weapon of the person who gave it.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| barking | "Aggressive" read from noise |
| approaching, or running toward, a person | Approach treated as charge |
| the animal's breed | Breed as threat |
| *(settled 2026-10-22)* Size is **not** barred. `[Ranadriel]` Mass is the definitive, evidentiary likelihood vector. With species, it sets the evolutionary damage output threshold (§ 1.18) — the heaviest class of harm an attacking animal can put at stake against the legal actor. | Breed stays barred: it is a label, not a measurement |
| the owner reaching for, holding, or calling the animal | The Palestine facts, as Ranadriel judged them: Max trotting and curious, not charging |
| the animal entering a room the official entered | Danger the official walked into |
| approaching with curiosity, sniffing, or jumping up | The officer's assumption of aggression, as Ranadriel judged the Palestine shooting |
| a nip or mouthing that does not break the skin | A play bite treated as an attack |
| presumed, when no attack command is recorded | `[Ranadriel]` the officer's alert posture is not justified where the owner gave no attack command |

### § 1.17 "Court" — *new; Ranadriel, 2026-10-22*
**Means** `[Open]` — defined by what it cannot be.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| `[Ranadriel]` a body raised while the nation is alive | Pseudo-courts and parallel courts of every kind, including the sovereign-citizen "common law courts" |
| `[Ranadriel]` a body without access to power and the tangible enforcement of its rulings | Courts in name only |
| `[Ranadriel]` a body whose judge cannot do the job | Titles without function |

**The bright line for "alive"** — `[Proposed — Seed]`: *A nation is alive while any court it constituted still sits.* Reconstitution (the collapse protocol, [LEDGER](./LEDGER.md)) can begin only when none does.

### § 1.18 "Evolutionary damage output threshold" — *new; Ranadriel's term, 2026-10-22*
**Means** the heaviest class of harm, on the Schedule of Weights, that an animal of a given **species** and **size class** can inflict on the **legal actor** — the official using force — as fixed in the schedule in [08](./08_THE_DAMAGES_LEDGER.md) (Rule L-5). Size class is read from the recording; the masses of animals of one species attacking together add.

| Is not | Closes |
|---|---|
| the animal's breed | Breed as threat — a label, not a measurement |
| the animal's reputation, or the reputation of its kind | Anecdote standing in for evidence |
| the animal's noise or approach before an attack | § 1.16 governs behavior |
| the official's fear or perception | "Perceived threat" (*Graham*), replaced by the schedule |
| set by a judge in a single case | The schedule is fixed in the text |
| applied to a venomous or toxic animal | Toxic animals are barred from the schedule and take their own entries `[Open]` |

## What Survives the Doubt

| Term | What it can be, once every "is not" is stripped |
|---|---|
| Official | Anyone exercising government power, by any route, in any role |
| Under color of office | On duty, or using the office |
| Violation | A breach of an article duty, or a constitutional deprivation — nothing more required |
| Injury | The listed harms, with no minimum |
| Knowledge | Awareness, or the recording |
| Accident | Harm with no violation — the only harm an official does not answer for, and the state still pays |
| Judicial act | Recorded, reviewable, disclosed in writing, and unbought |
| Immunity | Nothing, except the two protections the article itself grants |
| Employing government | Pays for every violation by its officials |
| Remedy | Exists by the article's own force |
| Court | Only while the nation it belongs to lives — or, once it has died, a body that holds real power, can enforce what it rules, and has a judge able to do the job |

## Rules of Construction

1. **No implied immunity.** No immunity shall be implied into this article.
2. **However named.** A rule excluded by this article stays excluded under any other name.
3. **No inference from omission.** The listing of any item in this article, or its omission from a list, implies nothing about any item not listed.
4. **No balancing.** Where this article states a rule, no court shall weigh it against any interest the article does not state.
5. **Words and definitions control.** This article is read by its words and the definitions in this section. No common-law background, structural inference, or claim of sovereignty narrows it.
6. **Ambiguity resolves toward the remedy.**
7. **No exception without a clock.** Every exception in this article runs on a documentary trigger and for a fixed period. No court may extend any deadline or excuse any consequence for good cause, in the interests of justice, or for any reason the article does not state. *(See [06_AUTOMATIC_LAWS.md](./06_AUTOMATIC_LAWS.md): the Speedy Trial Act's "ends of justice" exclusion is how an automatic law dies.)*
